## 2026-05-13 - GLSL Hash Function Bottlenecks
**Learning:** In GLSL shaders, using trigonometric functions (`tan`, `atan`, `cos`, `sin`) within hash and noise functions significantly increases Special Function Unit (SFU) usage. This creates a severe performance bottleneck, especially when these functions are called frequently (like in volumetric rendering or anti-aliasing loops). The codebase originally used these operations inside `hash12`, `hash13`, `hash14`, and `hash` functions.
**Action:** Always prefer pure ALU-based operations (like Dave Hoskins' hash methods) over trigonometric functions for pseudo-random number generation in GLSL. When writing or reviewing shader code, actively replace `fract(sin(...))` or `fract(tan(...))` with their ALU equivalents to minimize SFU instruction overhead and maximize throughput.
